Article 1 · best for LinkedIn + Medium · links to your benchmark page + cold email service
The cold email reply rates nobody wants to publish
Every cold email benchmark report you have read was published by a company that makes money when you believe cold email is easy. So the numbers are inflated, the methodology is hidden, and the takeaway is always "send more." Here is the honest version. A healthy B2B cold email campaign in 2026 gets a 2 to 4 percent total reply rate. Anything above 2 percent on genuinely cold traffic is good. The number that actually matters, though, is the positive reply rate: the share of sends that turn into a real, interested conversation. A strong campaign lands 0.3 to 0.5 percent positive replies. A clean 0.5 percent is elite. That is a beast of a campaign, not a starting point. Translate that into meetings and the picture gets honest fast. At strong performance it takes 250 to 500 well targeted sends to book one qualified meeting. On an average campaign, closer to 500 to 1,000. If your plan assumed a meeting per 50 emails, that is why your pipeline disappointed you. None of this means cold email is dead. It means it is a volume and precision game, not a magic trick. The teams that win do three boring things well: they keep the list tight, they keep deliverability clean, and they write like a human instead of a mail merge. One more thing the vendors will not tell you: open rate is over. Apple Mail Privacy Protection pre loads tracking pixels, so open rates are inflated to 60 to 80 percent and mean nothing. If a tool is still selling you on opens, close the tab. Article 2 · best for Medium + LinkedIn · links to your AI automation service
What an AI automation agency actually does, and what it should cost in 2026
"AI automation agency" has become one of those phrases that means everything and nothing. So here is the plain version of what the good ones actually do, and what you should pay. An AI automation agency builds custom systems that do work your team currently does by hand. Not a chatbot bolted onto your homepage. Real internal plumbing: lead research and enrichment, list building, outreach, CRM updates, onboarding, reporting. The unglamorous operational work that quietly eats your team's week. The "AI" part is specific. Large language models handle the judgment steps that a simple rules engine cannot: reading a company's site and summarizing what they do, deciding which of three follow ups fits a reply, cleaning messy data into something structured. The automation part is the pipes around it that move the work without a human babysitting each step. What separates a real agency from a reseller is ownership. A reseller logs into their own accounts, runs your work on their tools, and takes the whole system with them the day you stop paying. A real agency builds on your accounts, your CRM, your data, and hands you something you keep. One is a subscription you can never cancel. The other is an asset. On cost: a single well scoped automation usually lands in the low thousands to build. A full system across multiple workflows runs higher. Be suspicious of both ends. Anyone quoting a few hundred dollars is reselling a template. Anyone quoting a permanent fat retainer with no handover is renting you your own operations. If you rent your automation, you own nothing
There is a quiet trap in how most companies buy automation, and it does not show up until the day you try to leave. You hire an agency. They are good. Leads start coming in. The cold email system hums, the CRM updates itself, onboarding runs on rails. Then twelve months in you look under the hood and realize none of it is yours. The sending domains are registered to them. The lead lists live in their account. The automation logic sits in a workspace you cannot log into. Stop paying and the whole machine goes dark, and it leaves with them. That is not a partnership. That is a hostage situation with good onboarding. The alternative is simple to say and harder to find: build everything on your infrastructure. Your domains. Your inboxes. Your CRM. Your data. Your automation logic, documented so your team can run it after handover. The build costs more up front because you are creating an asset instead of renting access to one. Over any real time horizon it is cheaper, and far less fragile. The test is easy. Before you sign with anyone, ask one question: if we part ways tomorrow, what do we keep?
